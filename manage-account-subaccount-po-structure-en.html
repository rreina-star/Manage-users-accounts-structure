Manage — Working document

Account, Subaccount and PO

How Manage is organized today, where it splits between products and teams, and the proposal for one consistent structure the client sees.

01

Who uses Manage?

Manage doesn't show the same view to everyone. How much detail each point of contact (POC) sees depends on their role within the client's organization.

CEO
Scope: the entire accountSees all subaccounts, teams, and the consolidated month-over-month billing.
CFO
Scope: entire account, financial focusCompares costs by employee and by subaccount, month over month.
Department
Manager
Scope: subaccounts in their departmentRoster, PTO, and performance for their people.
Area Manager
Scope: one specific division or regionDoesn't see other areas within the same account.
Supervisor
Scope: one specific teamApproves PTO and views the roster only for their team.
Legal Advisor
Functional scope, not hierarchicalLegal documents and governance in the subaccounts they're granted access to — no billing access.

Unlike the other five roles, the Legal Advisor doesn't sit at one point on the ladder: they cut across by module, regardless of hierarchy level.

The base schema

Account, Subaccount, and Team group people. The PO groups billing charges. They're two independent logics that may — or may not — line up.

Same client, five different hierarchies — and none of them maps 1:1 to the client's real structure.

Hierarchy level isn't the only variable: scope is also defined by division and by function — a division might be a location, a business line, or whatever way the account organizes itself internally. The examples below are illustrative.

In a small account, you don't always need to create subaccounts or teams: the Account works directly as the Team. There, the Supervisor ends up seeing the same thing an Area Manager would see in a large account — because there are no further levels below to narrow it down.

Manage, Hire, Placement, Admin Tech, and Admin Ops share the same tree. The PO stays as an independent billing layer.
